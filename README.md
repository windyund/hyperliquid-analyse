**Hyperliquid whale tracking + on-chain analytics. The leaderboard tells you who's making money. HyperIntel tells you who's worth following.**

**Why "profitable ≠ worth copying"**

Hyperliquid's leaderboard ranks purely by realized PnL — but the top of that board is a mix of three very different traders:

- 🎰 **The one-hit gamblers** — went all-in once, hit big, but have no repeatable edge. Next trade could give it all back.
- ♻️ **The Martingale whales** — brute-forcing it with deep pockets, averaging down over and over. Looks great on paper until the liquidation hits. They have the capital to survive drawdowns you can't — not copyable for a regular trader.
- ✅ **The actually-worth-following** — returns aren't flashy, but drawdowns are small, track record is long, style is consistent, Sharpe ratio is solid.

The problem: that single leaderboard number can't tell these three apart. That's what HyperIntel does.

**Three key differentiators**

1. **Screens for real skill, not account balance** — every whale is scored on mark-to-market performance, drawdown, Sharpe ratio, and 90+ day win rate — not a flattering realized-PnL headline.
2. **Risk signals, right up front** — Martingale patterns, wash trading, and market-maker wallets get auto-flagged in red — not buried behind a single ranking number.
3. **Quantified copyability** — their profit ≠ your profit. Wallets backed by heavy capital or signals too slow to follow get explicitly marked "not copyable," with an estimate of what a follower could actually capture after latency and slippage.
